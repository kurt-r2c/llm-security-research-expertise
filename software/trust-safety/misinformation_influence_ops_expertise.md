## 1. The Discipline — Why This Is Cybersecurity

### The narrative attack surface

Most security programs operate around three substrates: code (vulnerabilities),
accounts (credentials, sessions, authorization), and infrastructure
(networks, hosts, cloud). Influence operations exploit a fourth: *narrative* —
the public discourse around an institution and how it propagates through
algorithmic platforms.

Treating influence operations as outside cybersecurity is a costume choice.
The threat actor is often the same — GRU Unit 26165 runs both hack-and-leak
operations and the Doppelganger amplification clusters; Iran's IRGC-aligned
actors run both Pegasus-style espionage and Endless Mayfly. The forensic
substrate (timestamps, language artifacts, infrastructure reuse) is similar.
What differs is the payload: instead of a beacon calling C2, a tweet calling
for engagement; instead of credentials harvested, opinions moved. The defense
substrate (graph-based account-network analysis, behavioral fingerprinting,
content provenance) overlaps almost entirely with the detection-engineering,
anti-fraud, and threat-intel stacks.

Cross-link to `threat_intel_methodology_expertise.md` (the intel cycle is
structurally identical), `anti_fraud_abuse_engineering_expertise.md` §7 (CIB
as a class of fake-account abuse), and `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md`
§7 (AI-generated content is the supply-side of synthetic media).

### The four-category taxonomy

The field's working vocabulary distinguishes:

- **Misinformation** — false information shared without intent to deceive. The
  innocent retweet of a hoax. Most online "misinformation" is, in this strict
  sense, misinformation: ordinary users believing and sharing. Mitigation is
  educational, not punitive.
- **Disinformation** — false information created or shared *with intent to
  deceive*. The state-actor-authored fake news article; the synthetic image
  positioned as a real photograph. Intent is load-bearing; without it, the
  category is misinformation.
- **Malinformation** — true information weaponized through selective release,
  framing, or recontextualization. A real leaked email republished outside its
  context to imply a false narrative. The Macron Leaks 2017 mix of real and
  synthetic documents was a worked example. Malinformation is the category
  most resistant to fact-checking because fact-checkers cannot dispute what is
  true.
- **Influence operation** — a *coordinated campaign* by an actor (typically
  state-aligned or commercially-contracted) employing some mix of misinfo,
  disinfo, malinfo, behavior manipulation (inauthentic engagement,
  amplification), and asset acquisition (purchased followings, hijacked
  accounts) to shift public opinion or behavior. The unit of analysis. Single
  posts are not influence operations; networks of coordinated activity are.

Some practitioners add a fifth category, *information warfare*, distinguishing
state-on-state strategic campaigns conducted as part of military doctrine. The
distinction is real but the boundary porous; this doc uses "influence
operation" to cover both.

### Why traditional InfoSec underweights this

Three reasons: **no CVE issues** (no software defect, no patch, no
vulnerability-management on-ramp); **no SOC alert** (a network of 10,000
coordinated accounts generates no firewall, EDR, or SIEM event — the signal
lives in platform-internal telemetry the security team rarely accesses);
**no clear blast-radius accounting** (a successful influence op produces
shifted opinion and eroded trust, none of which is countable on the
incident-review timescale).

The mature program treats influence operations as a *peer discipline* —
owned adjacent to (not inside) the cybersecurity org, with formal interfaces.
Platform T&S teams own this work operationally; corporate-security owns
executive-protection and brand-impersonation; legal and comms own regulatory
and crisis-response. The CISO ensures none of the four categories gets
orphaned.

### Where influence operations land in a security program

- **Brand impersonation** — fake accounts impersonating company brand or
  executives across platforms; phishing, fraud, harassment, market-manipulation
  motives. Mitigation: continuous brand monitoring, platform-T&S takedown
  relationships, C2PA adoption for owned media.
- **Executive personal-account hardening** — public-facing executives are
  high-value targets for impersonation, swatting, deepfake-hostage scenarios,
  and account compromise.
- **Financial-market influence** — synthetic news (Pentagon explosion fake,
  May 2023, briefly moved S&P 500), pump-and-dump, short-seller-driven
  narrative campaigns.
- **Election integrity** for organizations adjacent to electoral
  infrastructure.
- **Employee safety** — the Arup deepfake-CFO case (Hong Kong, January 2024,
  HK$200M / US$25M wired across 15 transactions after a video call featuring
  deepfake renders of the CFO and colleagues).
- **Crisis communication** — influence operations frequently land during
  incidents; PR and security need a joint runbook.

## 2. Frameworks and Taxonomies

The field has standardized on a small number of frameworks. Knowing which
applies where is the prerequisite to writing or consuming public reports.

### The ABC Framework (Camille François, September 2019)

Three dimensions every influence operation has:

- **Actors** — *who* is conducting the operation. Manipulative actors with
  clear intent to disrupt democratic processes or the information ecosystem
  (typical examples: state intelligence services, state-aligned commercial
  contractors, ideological movements, financially motivated operators).
  Distinct from organic users behind whom actors hide.
- **Behaviors** — *how* the operation is conducted. Deceptive tactics and
  techniques: coordination, automation, inauthentic identity, asset
  acquisition (purchased accounts, hijacked accounts, AI-generated profile
  photos), cross-platform amplification, paid promotion as organic, time-
  coordinated posting. The Behavior axis is the platform-defined enforcement
  surface: most platform policies prohibit specific *behaviors* (CIB, scaled
  spam, inauthenticity) regardless of *content*, because behavior-based rules
  are content-neutral and survive partisan critique.
- **Content** — *what* is being said. Harmful content pushed to undermine
  individuals, organizations, or the public interest: false claims,
  decontextualized media, hate speech, harassment, doxing. Content is the
  most legally and politically contentious axis.

The framework's load-bearing insight: each axis admits a different category
of intervention. Actor-level interventions are sanctions, indictments,
diplomatic action. Behavior-level interventions are platform enforcement
(CIB takedowns) — content-neutral and durable. Content-level interventions
are moderation, labeling, fact-checking — content-dependent and contentious.
A regulatory regime that targets only one axis (e.g., content-moderation laws
without behavior rules) will systematically under-perform.

A 2020 Brookings extension (Alexandre Alaphilippe) added "**D**istribution"
as a fourth axis — the role of algorithmic amplification systems. Worth
knowing; not universally adopted.

### DISARM (and its predecessor AMITT)

The MITRE ATT&CK analog for influence operations. Originally developed as
**AMITT** (Adversarial Misinformation and Influence Tactics and Techniques) by
the COGSec / Misinfosec Working Group inside the Credibility Coalition,
2018–2020. Rebranded as **DISARM** (Disinformation Analysis and Risk
Management) under the DISARM Foundation, with active maintenance and
EU-institution adoption from 2022 onward.

DISARM comprises two paired frameworks:

- **DISARM Red** — offensive TTPs. The full lifecycle from *Plan Strategy* and
  *Plan Objectives* through *Develop Narratives*, *Develop Content*,
  *Establish Social Assets*, *Establish Legitimacy*, *Microtarget*, *Select
  Channels and Affordances*, *Conduct Pump Priming*, *Deliver Content*,
  *Maximize Exposure*, *Drive Online Harms*, *Drive Offline Activity*,
  *Persist in the Information Environment*, *Assess Effectiveness*. Modeled
  on the ATT&CK Tactic-Technique-Sub-technique structure with stable IDs.
- **DISARM Blue** — countermeasures. Each Red technique has zero-or-more
  Blue mitigations cataloged, with similar ID stability.

DISARM has STIX 2.1 bindings; the OASIS CTI TC has been moving toward formal
inclusion. EU institutions (EDMO, EEAS) and most rigorous public CIB writeups
since 2023 explicitly map findings to DISARM technique IDs the way TIRs map
to ATT&CK. For a security team approaching influence-ops adjacent work, the
DISARM-to-ATT&CK lift is small: same shape, different substrate.

### Other relevant frameworks

- **MITRE ATT&CK extension proposals** — ATT&CK proper has not been extended
  to influence-ops; DISARM-as-sibling-framework won the design battle.
  Cross-framework crosswalks exist.
- **MITRE ATLAS** (`atlas.mitre.org`) — adversarial-ML threat catalog. Several
  techniques overlap with influence-ops (AML.T0048 *External Harms*). Cross-
  link `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §7.
- **OASIS STIX 2.1** — the same data model used in technical CTI supports
  influence-ops via existing object types with DISARM attack-pattern objects
  substituted for ATT&CK. The OASIS CTI TC *Disrupting Disinformation*
  subgroup has been working since 2022 on extensions; some MISP galaxies are
  in production.
- **EU DSA Code of Practice on Disinformation** — platform-side framework
  rather than analytic. Voluntary 2018; integrated into DSA framework as of
  1 July 2025. Imposes commitments on VLOPs / VLOSEs under DSA Article 35
  (systemic-risk mitigation): ad placement, political-advertising
  transparency, integrity of services, user empowerment, researcher access,
  transparency reporting. Cross-link §11.
- **NATO StratCom COE / FIMI taxonomy** — Riga-based; *Foreign Information
  Manipulation and Interference*, the EU-preferred term. Roughly synonymous
  with state-sponsored influence operations but excludes domestic actors.

### The "framework tax"

Every framework is lossy. ABC collapses to behavior-axis intervention; DISARM
presupposes discrete-stage campaigns (many real operations are continuous).
Frameworks are scaffolds, not substitutes. The flag of a bad writeup is
forcing an incident into a framework that doesn't fit; the mature analyst
names the mismatch rather than papering over it.

## 3. Coordinated Inauthentic Behavior (CIB) Detection

### Platform-defined CIB criteria

CIB is the operational term-of-art for coordinated inauthentic activity that
violates platform rules independent of content. Meta's definition (from the
2018 launch of the CIB enforcement category) is the de facto industry
baseline: *the use of multiple accounts to mislead people about who is behind
the activity, in violation of platform Community Standards*. Twitter/X has a
similar *Manipulating Twitter* policy; YouTube uses *deceptive practices*;
TikTok uses *integrity and authenticity* policies.

The criteria distill to three intersecting conditions:

1. **Coordination.** The accounts act together — synchronized posting,
   cross-posting identical content, reciprocal engagement, shared
   infrastructure. Coordination distinguishes CIB from organic mass
   behavior; without it, you have a viral post, not an operation.
2. **Inauthenticity.** The accounts misrepresent their origin — fake
   profile photos (often GAN-generated), fabricated bio details, claimed
   identity at odds with operating-location, purchased aged accounts, hijacked
   accounts repurposed. Inauthenticity distinguishes CIB from organic
   coordination (e.g., a grassroots campaign with real users posting on
   instruction); the underlying behavior may be similar but the policy lever
   different.
3. **Behavior.** The activity is the actionable surface — not the content.
   Platforms enforce against the behavior pattern even when the content
   itself is permissible (or even true). This is the load-bearing design
   choice: behavior-based rules survive partisan and political scrutiny in
   ways content-based rules don't.

### Network-graph analysis

The foundational analytic technique. Build an account-by-account graph from
platform data; edges encoded as follows-relationships, engagement (replies,
likes, retweets), shared-content (identical or near-identical posts), and
co-mention. Coordinated operations cluster densely because their internal
activity is structurally over-connected — accounts in a CIB cluster engage
each other at rates orders of magnitude above the platform baseline.

Graphika's signature analytic deliverable is a network-graph render with
modularity-based community detection; the visual immediately distinguishes a
coordinated cluster (dense internal edges, sparse external edges) from
organic communities (gradient density). Force-directed layouts (Fruchterman-
Reingold, ForceAtlas2) make this legible. The 2016 IRA network rendered in
Graphika's post-takedown analyses is the canonical visual — a tight cluster
of operator accounts surrounded by sparse halos of real-user engagement.

Tools: **Graphika ATLAS** (commercial; the dominant platform); **Gephi**
(open-source graph visualization, free for academic use); **Maltego** (CTI-
side graph platform with custom transforms for platform-API data);
**Brandwatch / Talkwalker / Meltwater** (commercial social-listening
platforms with some graph capability); **Hoaxy** (Indiana OSoMe, open-
source, for low-credibility content spread).

### Behavioral fingerprinting

Beyond raw graph structure, account behavior produces signatures:

- **Temporal posting patterns.** Bot accounts post at machine-regular cadence
  or burst-on-trigger; humans post on circadian rhythms with noise.
  Posting-time histograms of CIB clusters typically show *machine bimodal*
  patterns (e.g., bursts at the operator-time-zone working hours, near-zero
  outside) or *flat* patterns (24/7 with no diurnal cycle) — both
  diagnostic.
- **Content overlap.** Identical or near-identical posts across accounts
  ("copy-pasta amplification") is the simplest signature. Jaccard-similarity
  on tokenized text catches this at scale.
- **Narrative timing.** Coordinated push of the same narrative across accounts
  within a narrow window — the first hour of a news event, for example —
  signals pre-prepared talking points.
- **Engagement reciprocity.** The fake-accounts-engaging-each-other-to-
  manufacture-the-appearance-of-organic-conversation pattern. Reciprocity
  graphs (account A engages account B AND account B engages account A) show
  abnormal clustering inside CIB clusters.
- **Hashtag and link-share patterns.** A small cluster of accounts driving
  most of the use of a particular hashtag or link, especially during the
  first hours after introduction.
- **Profile-photo provenance.** Reverse-image-searchable photos lifted from
  real social media; AI-generated GAN faces with characteristic artifacts
  (asymmetric earrings, distorted backgrounds, fixed eye-position in
  StyleGAN2 outputs); duplicate profile photos across accounts.

### Linguistic fingerprinting

The text content of an operation carries signatures beyond the message:

- **Machine-translation artifacts.** Russian-original content posted in
  English with characteristic patterns: missing or hyper-corrected articles,
  syntactic constructions (subject-final structures), preposition errors
  ("on the past week"), idiom literalism, anglicized Russian punctuation
  (Cyrillic ellipsis Unicode point, paragraph indent vs. blank line).
  Endless Mayfly content had distinctive Iranian-English MT patterns;
  Doppelganger content has characteristic Russian-MT patterns.
- **Stylometric analysis.** Word-length distribution, sentence-length
  distribution, function-word frequency, punctuation patterns. Operators
  often have stable per-operator signatures, so multi-operator clusters fall
  apart under stylometric clustering. *Mendenhall*, *Burrows Delta*, and
  modern transformer-embedding-based methods all work; stylometry is mature
  literature with a 30-year track record.
- **Named-entity overlap.** Across-account use of the same named entities
  (people, organizations, locations) at rates above baseline. Particularly
  diagnostic when paired with consistent stance toward the entity.
- **Topic-model overlap.** LDA-based topic modeling on per-account post
  history; CIB accounts cluster topically because they were briefed off
  shared talking points.

### Asset acquisition patterns

How operators acquire accounts is itself a signature.

- **Aged accounts.** Accounts created years before the operation begins, idle
  or low-volume until activation. Indicates purchased accounts from
  underground markets, or accounts cultivated on long timelines. The 2016
  IRA operation used both — some accounts had been quietly building
  followings since 2014.
- **Compromised accounts.** Hijacked-via-credential-stuffing real-user
  accounts repurposed. Detectable by sudden change in posting behavior,
  topic, language, or geography for an established account. The 2024
  Doppelganger campaign relied on this heavily for its X (Twitter)
  amplification layer.
- **AI-generated personas.** Accounts created from scratch with GAN-generated
  profile photos, scraped or LLM-generated bio text, and synthetic personal-
  history. Tank-Man-synthesized class. Detection: reverse-image searches that
  return no hits combined with GAN-artifact heuristics on the profile photo.
- **Purchased boosts.** Real users paid (via micro-tasking platforms — Yandex
  Toloka, Amazon Mechanical Turk, Telegram channels) to perform engagement
  actions. Less detectable in network structure (real users, real devices,
  real behaviors) but detectable in *engagement velocity at activation*
  (sudden spike, then return to baseline).

### The "boosting" detection class — paid vs. organic

A specific CIB sub-class deserves its own treatment: *boosted* content that
purchased its initial amplification while pretending to be organic. The
ABC-framework Behavior axis catches this when:

- A small cluster of accounts produces the *first hour* of engagement on a
  post,
- The cluster's accounts have low organic-engagement baselines,
- The post's later engagement (after the artificial boost has passed
  threshold) drives algorithmic recommendation,
- The post's amplification looks "organic" in aggregate but its trajectory
  was set by the artificial boost.

Detection is structurally similar to early-stage botnet detection — the bot
gives away the operation at activation, before its noise blends into baseline
traffic. Platforms operate friction at this layer; researchers infer from
trajectory analysis on the public timeline.

### Hamilton 2.0 and the open-source toolkit

Hamilton 2.0 (German Marshall Fund Alliance for Securing Democracy) tracks
~500 Russia-, China-, and Iran-state-aligned Twitter accounts and surfaces
their narrative topics, hashtag use, and amplification patterns daily. The
dashboard's selection methodology is publicly documented; the data is
public-API-derived. The tool is the reference for "what are state-aligned
accounts saying right now"; the methodology document is the reference for
how to constitute such a panel rigorously.

shape (rules, scoring, FP analysis, evolution under adversary adaptation) is
identical to detection-engineering work, just on a different telemetry
substrate.

## 4. State-Affiliated Influence Operations Corpus

The empirical core of the discipline. Reference catalog by actor; see §6 for
event-by-event case studies.

### Russia

The longest-running and most-documented influence-operation actor space.

- **Internet Research Agency (IRA)** — Saint Petersburg troll farm founded by
  Yevgeny Prigozhin (killed 2023). Operated 2013–2023; rebranded after the
  2023 Wagner mutiny. Conducted the canonical 2016 US election operation
  (Mueller indictment, SSCI reports). Successor orgs (see Storm-1516) inherited
  staff and infrastructure.
- **GRU Unit 26165 (APT28 / Fancy Bear)** — Russian military intelligence.
  Conducted the 2016 DNC and Podesta hack-and-leak; Macron Leaks 2017.
  France formally attributed to GRU APT28 in April 2025.
- **GRU Unit 74455 (Sandworm)** — destructive-operations focus (NotPetya,
  Olympic Destroyer, Ukraine grid attacks) with influence components.
- **Doppelganger** — commercial influence-op network active since 2022.
  Operates fake versions of real news outlets (Der Spiegel, Le Parisien, Fox
  News, Washington Post, Bild) plus hundreds of thousands of automated X
  accounts. Attributed by Meta (December 2022) to Russian companies *Structura*
  and *Social Design Agency*; confirmed by VIGINUM (June 2023); DOJ seized 32
  domains in 2024. Tied to Sergei Kiriyenko (First Deputy Chief of Staff,
  Russian Presidential Executive Office). Primary EU-targeting operation;
  heavily targeted the June 2024 EU Parliament elections.
- **Storm-1516** — Microsoft-tracked IRA successor. Produced two viral fake
  videos in the 2024 US cycle (Trump-rally-attendee attack hoax; Harris
  hit-and-run hoax). Operates 100+ pro-Russian news sites fronted by ex-Florida-
  cop John Dougan in Moscow.
- **CopyCop** — Insikt-tracked operation impersonating legitimate news outlets;
  94+ inauthentic German-language sites for the February 2025 cycle.
- **Operation Overload** / **Operation Undercut** — additional Insikt-tracked
  Russian clusters.

### China

Larger volume than Russian operations; lower per-message engagement and
political subtlety. Quality improved with AI-generated content adoption 2023–2025.

- **Spamouflage / Dragonbridge** — Mandiant-named and Google TAG-tracked
  China-aligned operation. Operates across YouTube, X, Facebook, TikTok,
  Reddit, niche platforms; tens of thousands of accounts. Themes: anti-US,
  anti-HK-protest, anti-Taiwan-independence, pro-PRC framing of US domestic
  controversies. Google disrupted 50,000+ instances in 2022 and 10,000+ in Q1
  2024 alone. Engagement remains poor — volume-not-influence. In April 2024,
  fake-American-persona accounts began posting US politics; some received real
  engagement (one retweeted by Alex Jones).
- **Storm-1376** — Microsoft-tracked PRC operation targeting January 2024
  Taiwan presidential election. AI-generated candidate audio, news anchors,
  the *Secret History of Tsai Ing-wen* AI ebook. First major state deployment
  of generative-AI in an election cycle. Cross-link §5.
- **MULAN / Canada Spamouflage transnational repression** — June 2024
  Government of Canada RRM report linked a Spamouflage-affiliated repression
  campaign in Canada to PRC actors.

### Iran

Smaller, deniable, with deletion-and-misdirect tradecraft.

- **Endless Mayfly** — Citizen Lab-attributed (May 2019) Iran-aligned network.
  Distinctive tradecraft: spoof legitimate news outlet, push fake story, then
  *delete the article and redirect the URL to the legitimate outlet's domain*
  after traction — leaving the impression that the legitimate outlet had
  published and retracted. Hence "Mayfly."
- **Liminal Panda** (CrowdStrike-named) and **Charming Kitten / APT35** —
  IRGC-aligned, primary espionage with influence-op adjacency.
- **Storm-2035** — Microsoft-tracked Iranian operation targeting the 2024 US
  cycle with cross-platform fake-American-persona accounts. Lower volume than
  Russian or Chinese ops.

### Other actors

- **Saudi Arabia / UAE** — *Falcon Eye* / *Project Raven* (UAE-aligned,
  espionage + influence components). Saudi operations against
  Khashoggi-adjacent journalists, dissidents, Qatar during the 2017–2021 GCC
  crisis. Pegasus is the high-confidence attribution corner; influence-op
  corner less crisp.
- **North Korea** — BlueNoroff and Lazarus have periodic influence-op adjacency;
  primary mission financial / destructive.
- **Israel-Hamas-Lebanon-Palestine** — 2023–present produced the largest
  cross-cutting synthetic-media corpus in any single event. Hamas/Hezbollah-
  aligned campaigns; Israeli state and contractor operations (STOIC, May 2024
  takedown by Meta/OpenAI); Iran-aligned amplification; massive recontextualized
  and synthetic content volume.
- **US-aligned and Western-democracies' operations** — pre-2013 documented
  covert USIA / VOA activity; Snowden-era JTRIG (GCHQ); May 2024 STOIC
  takedown documented an Israeli commercial contractor; Reuters reported a
  US-DoD-contracted operation in the Middle East and Central Asia. Smaller
  public corpus, not zero.

### Ben Nimmo quarterly reports as ongoing source

Since joining OpenAI in 2023, Ben Nimmo has continued the quarterly-report
discipline established at DFRLab / Graphika / Meta. The OpenAI *Disrupting
Malicious Uses of AI* reports (semi-annual since 2024) are now the most-
authoritative single source for state-aligned LLM use in influence operations.
Treat as canonical primary-source for AI-content adoption curve in
influence-ops over 2024–2026.

## 5. AI-Generated Content and Synthetic Media

The largest structural shift in influence operations since the rise of social
platforms. The 2022–2024 generative-AI inflection moved synthetic media from
specialist capability to commodity capability across text, image, video, and
voice.

### Text generation

LLMs (GPT-4 family, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek) have
moved per-token disinformation generation cost effectively to zero. The
contemporary influence-op corpus shows LLM-generated content adoption from
late 2022 onward.

Operational implications:

- **Volume scaling.** Operations that previously needed human writers can
  now generate thousands of variant posts daily. The 2024 Doppelganger X
  amplification layer is almost entirely LLM-generated text.
- **Per-language localization.** A single Russian-operator team can run
  English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, and Polish operations
  simultaneously by routing through LLM translation+localization. Detection-
  side: language artifacts that previously indicated Russian-MT now indicate
  a specific LLM-generation pattern, and the underlying operator may be
  invisible.
- **Persona maintenance.** LLM-generated bio text, post histories, and
  comment threads sustain inauthentic accounts. The "GPT-stylometric
  fingerprint" of base models is detectable but progressively less reliable
  as operators move to fine-tuned or instruction-tuned variants.
- **Conversational amplification.** Real-time LLM-driven replies to
  organic users — the conversational version of bot amplification — appeared
  in Doppelganger's 2023–2024 X campaigns. The Microsoft and Meta CIB
  takedown reports document the pattern.

Detection (cross-link `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §10):
DetectGPT, GLTR, Hugging Face detectors. All declining in reliability as
models improve. The watermarking thread (Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023)
embeds a statistical signal in generated text that survives moderate
paraphrase; OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic have all signaled work
on watermarking; deployment has been partial and slow. The arms-race
dynamic favors the generator.

### Image generation

Midjourney v5+, Flux.1 family, Stable Diffusion XL/3, DALL-E 3, Gemini
Imagen 3, FLUX Pro. The 2023 "Pentagon explosion" fake image (May 22, 2023)
that briefly moved the S&P 500 is the canonical financial-market influence
incident. Tank-Man-synthesized AI-generated portrait photos populate the
profile-photo layer of thousands of inauthentic accounts across operations.

Detection: forensic artifacts (anatomical inconsistencies, asymmetric
accessories, text-rendering errors, characteristic Stable Diffusion noise
patterns in early-2023 generations) have largely been engineered out by
2025 model generations. The detection backstop is now *provenance*: signed
metadata from cameras and image-generation pipelines (see C2PA below).

### Video generation

The 2024 inflection point. OpenAI Sora (announced February 2024), Google Veo
3, Runway Gen-3, Pika 1.0+, Kling, Luma Dream Machine. Real-time generation
is not quite there in 2026, but pre-generated synthetic video is
production-capable for 5-to-60-second clips. The Taiwan 2024 election cycle
saw the first major state deployment of AI-generated video in an election
context — the *Storm-1376* operation produced synthetic news anchors,
synthetic candidate audio matched to lip-synced video, and the AI-generated
*Secret History of Tsai Ing-wen* ebook with accompanying video promotional
materials.

The 2024 US election cycle saw narrower deployment than feared — most
viral synthetic political video in the US cycle was identified and labeled
within hours. The deepfake panic exceeded the realized deepfake harm.
However, the substrate for the next cycle exists.

### Voice cloning

ElevenLabs (the dominant commercial vendor), OpenAI Voice Engine (limited
release), Microsoft VALL-E (research), Meta SeamlessM4T. Voice cloning is
the most-deployed-against-individuals AI capability in the corpus.

- **The Arup case** (Hong Kong, January 2024, disclosed May 2024). Finance
  worker convinced over a video call featuring deepfake renders of CFO and
  multiple colleagues; wired HK$200M (US$25M) across 15 transactions. The
  perpetrators trained the deepfakes on publicly-available video of the
  victims from past conferences and Zoom recordings. The first public
  $25M+ deepfake-CFO incident. Cross-link
  `webrtc_realtime_comms_expertise.md` §6.
- **Robocalls** — January 2024 New Hampshire primary featured a deepfake
  Biden voice robocall telling Democratic voters not to vote. The
  responsible operator (Steve Kramer, a political operative) was indicted;
  fines totaled US$6M (FCC) plus state charges. The first US-election-cycle
  deepfake-voice incident with named operator.
- **Spear-phishing escalation.** Voice cloning from a 3-second sample is
  now within consumer reach. The IT-helpdesk-impersonation-via-deepfake-CISO
  scenario has been demonstrated in red-team exercises and is presumed in
  the wild.

### Synthetic identities for inauthentic accounts

GAN-generated profile photos remain the workhorse. ThisPersonDoesNotExist.com
(NVIDIA StyleGAN2, then StyleGAN3) democratized the technique. Detection:
forensic artifacts (StyleGAN2 fixes eye position to specific coordinates,
which is detectable; StyleGAN3 addresses this), reverse-image search returns
no hits (real photos return hits; GAN-generated photos do not), and modern
diffusion-model-based portraits have their own characteristic artifacts.

### C2PA — the defensive provenance primitive

The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA), founded 2021,
with Adobe, Microsoft, Truepic, Sony, Canon, Nikon, Leica, BBC, Intel,
NVIDIA, Google as founding members. Specification 2.2 (May 2025); 2.3 draft
as of May 2026; supports JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, HEIC, MP4, MOV, PDF, MP3, WAV.

A **C2PA Manifest** is a digitally signed bundle of *assertions* (camera
model, capture time, geolocation if disclosed, edit history, generative-AI
tool if any, signer identity) embedded in the asset and verifiable against a
public-key trust list. Producers (cameras, editing software, generative-AI
vendors) sign on output; consumers (browsers, platforms) verify on display.

Adoption (May 2026): Adobe Creative Cloud signs by default. Microsoft Windows
and Edge verify and surface manifest UI. Leica, Sony, Nikon ship signing in
pro-tier camera models; Google Pixel 9+ signs. OpenAI DALL-E 3, Sora; Adobe
Firefly; Microsoft Designer; Bing Image Creator sign. Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok,
X support partial verify-and-label. EU AI Act Article 50 effectively mandates
this trajectory for EU-distributed AI-generated content from August 2026.
Cross-link §11.

Limitations: C2PA establishes *provenance*, not *truth* — a signed photo can
still depict a staged scene. Manifests are strippable; unattributed media is
not necessarily synthetic, just unprovenanced. Adoption is asymmetric.
Cross-link `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §10 and World Privacy
Forum analysis on manifest-data privacy implications.

### DARPA SemaFor and detection-side R&D

DARPA *Semantic Forensics* (SemaFor) ran 2021–September 2024 — the
US-government R&D vehicle for synthetic-media detection, focused on
semantic-level inconsistencies (vs. pixel-level forensics, which predecessor
MediFor covered). Technology transitioned to UL Research Institutes' *Digital
Safety Research Institute* (DSRI); commercial partners include Reality
Defender and Truepic. *AI Forensics Open Research Challenge Evaluation* (AI
FORCE) is the follow-on community challenge.

Commercial detection vendors: Reality Defender, Truepic, Sensity AI, Hive
Moderation, Optic, DeepMedia.ai. As of May 2026, no detector achieves better
than 80% on contemporary SOTA generations across modalities. Defender lag
(6–18 months) is structural; treat detectors as *signaling*, not *verifying*.

## 6. Real Influence Operations Corpus

A canonical reference catalog of named incidents. Each entry: actor,
technique, attribution, lesson.

### 2016 US Presidential Election

**Actor:** Internet Research Agency (Russia, Prigozhin-funded); GRU Unit
26165 (APT28).
**Technique:** Multi-platform IRA operation — ~3,000 Facebook ads, ~80,000
organic posts, ~3,400 Twitter accounts (including @TEN_GOP impersonating
the Tennessee Republican Party with 130K+ followers), Instagram (est. 187M
impressions), YouTube, Tumblr. Real-world organizing via Facebook (August
2016 Houston "Heart of Texas" / "United Muslims of America" dueling
protest). GRU parallel hack-and-leak — DNC and Podesta emails via DCLeaks,
Guccifer 2.0, WikiLeaks.
**Attribution:** Definitive — US IC Assessment (January 2017); Mueller
indictment of 13 Russians (February 2018); SSCI Vol. 2 report (October
2019, with significant DiResta input from New Knowledge).
**Lesson:** The defining modern incident; established the methodological
playbook (network-graph analysis, case-study reconstruction, cross-platform
coordination as unit-of-analysis). Most subsequent work is calibrated
against this reference corpus.

### Brexit Referendum 2016

**Actor:** Russia-aligned IRA + *Cambridge Analytica* / SCL Group
(illegal-Facebook-data psychographic targeting on 87M users, Mercer-funded,
used by both Trump 2016 and Brexit Leave.EU).
**Attribution:** UK ICO investigation confirmed CA's data misuse; Russian
piece less crisp. UK Parliament DCMS Committee report (2019).
**Lesson:** First documented combination of state-aligned CIB and
commercial-political microtargeting on a single event; the *data-grievance
pathway* (Aleksandr Kogan's `thisisyourdigitallife` → SCL/CA) is canonical.

### Macron Leaks 2017

**Actor:** GRU APT28 (Fancy Bear).
**Technique:** Hack-and-leak. ~20,000 Macron campaign emails leaked two
days before the second round; genuine documents mixed with synthetic
forgeries to seed doubt; amplified via 4chan and pro-Trump networks.
**Attribution:** Flashpoint moderate-confidence (2017); France formal
attribution to GRU APT28 in April 2025.
**Lesson:** Canonical *malinformation* worked example (real documents
weaponized through selective release with synthetic seeding). *No
measurable effect* — Macron won by 32%. The French defensive playbook
(48-hour pre-vote information blackout; Macron campaign pre-seeding fake
docs to detect leak sourcing) is the reference.

### Hong Kong Protests 2019

**Actor:** China-aligned (PRC MSS-adjacent suspected).
**Technique:** Twitter/Facebook accounts pushing pro-Beijing content
characterizing protesters as ISIS-equivalent or paid US agents. Twitter
suspended 936 accounts; Facebook removed multiple pages/groups/accounts.
**Lesson:** First explicit platform-attribution of Chinese state-aligned
CIB; established the *takedown-and-public-attribution* pattern.

### COVID-19 Disinformation (2020–2022)

**Actors:** Russia (anti-mRNA framing, pro-Sputnik V), China (lab-leak
counter-narratives), Iran, domestic anti-vaccine movements. Mixed state /
organic; hard to disentangle.
**Attribution:** EU EEAS East StratCom Task Force; SIO *Virality Project*;
CISA reporting.
**Lesson:** First true *infodemic* (WHO's coinage). Health-information
manipulation became a permanent platform-T&S category. The Virality
Project became a flashpoint for political attacks on SIO that contributed
to the 2024 wind-down.

### US 2020 Election + January 6

**Actors:** Primarily domestic ("Stop the Steal" influencer ecosystem);
secondary state-actor amplification.
**Technique:** *Long Fuse* (per EIP report) — slow accumulation of
false-claims-about-voting before the election, ignition into the "stolen
election" metanarrative post-election, culminating in January 6. Right-
leaning blue-check influencers transformed one-off incidents into cohesive
systemic-fraud narratives.
**Attribution:** EIP report (March 2021); January 6 Select Committee
final report (December 2022).
**Lesson:** First large incident dominated by *domestic* actors. Domestic
disinformation lives in First Amendment protected territory in the US;
platform-T&S enforcement engaging it directly became the political
vulnerability that eroded the Stanford / Harvard / UW research consortium
2022–2024.

### Ukraine Invasion 2022–Present

**Actors:** Russia (Doppelganger, IRA successors, Storm-1516, RT/Sputnik
fronts); Ukrainian and Western counter-narrative.
**Technique:** Continuous narrative warfare; anti-Ukraine framings for
European audiences (refugee discontent, energy-cost narratives,
corruption frames); Doppelganger fake-news-site network; Telegram-heavy.
**Attribution:** EU DisinfoLab *Doppelganger Hub*; multi-jurisdiction
government attribution; September 2024 DOJ Tenet Media indictment (two
RT employees paying six US conservative influencers ~$10M).
**Lesson:** Reference case for *continuous* (not event-driven) influence
operations. Tenet Media is the first public US example of paid-influencer
state-aligned operations with named US recipients.

### Israel-Hamas 2023–Present

**Actors:** Hamas / Hezbollah-aligned; Israeli state and contractor
(STOIC); Iran-aligned; organic content surge.
**Technique:** Largest cross-cutting synthetic-media corpus in any single
contemporary event. Recontextualized media (real footage from Syria 2014
presented as Gaza 2023), AI-generated casualty images, deepfake audio of
leaders. 2024 STOIC operation (Israeli contractor, ~500 fake accounts
targeting US audiences pro-Israel) dismantled by Meta + OpenAI May 2024.
**Lesson:** Worked example of how AI-generated content scales an
information-warfare environment when state and non-state actors adopt
simultaneously. Detection capacity outpaced by content velocity in early
quarters.

### Taiwan 2024 Election

**Actor:** PRC state-aligned (Storm-1376 per Microsoft).
**Technique:** First major election-cycle deployment of AI-generated video
and audio by a state actor. Synthetic news anchors; deepfake audio of
candidates; AI-generated *Secret History of Tsai Ing-wen* ebook; AI-
manipulated videos targeting Lai Ching-te.
**Lesson:** Taiwan's resilience response is the canonical *whole-of-
society* defense — media-literacy programs (Taiwan FactCheck Center,
MyGoPen, Cofacts), in-school pre-bunking, sovereign-AI infrastructure
(TAIDE), rapid government communication. Despite intensive interference,
Lai won.

### US 2024 Election

**Actors:** Russia (Storm-1516, Doppelganger, Tenet Media); Iran
(Storm-2035); China (Spamouflage); domestic.
**Technique:** First US presidential election in the post-generative-AI
substrate. Deepfake Biden robocall (NH, January 2024); Storm-1516 viral
fake videos (Trump-rally-attendee attack hoax; Harris hit-and-run hoax);
DOJ Tenet Media indictment (September 2024).
**Attribution:** Microsoft MTAC election reports (5+ in cycle); DOJ
indictments; Meta Adversarial Threat Reports.
**Lesson:** Realized deepfake harm < predicted deepfake harm. Most
synthetic content was identified and labeled within hours; no "deepfake
October surprise." Larger impact came from longer-running narrative
campaigns. Recalibrate accordingly, while still expecting significant
AI-content deployment in 2028.

### EU Parliament 2024 / German Federal 2025

**Actor:** Russia (Doppelganger primary, CopyCop, Operation Overload).
**Technique:** Continuous fake-news-site network + social amplification;
Insikt Group identified 94+ inauthentic German-language sites for the
February 2025 cycle alone.
**Lesson:** Worked example of *DSA-era* response. January 2025
Code-of-Practice integration created concrete enforcement levers; Meta,
X, TikTok face EU proceedings tied partly to disinformation response.

## 7. Platform Integrity Engineering

The operational discipline that runs inside major platforms (Meta, X,
TikTok, Google/YouTube, Reddit, Discord, Snap, LinkedIn) and increasingly
inside major generative-AI vendors (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta
AI, Mistral, xAI). The institutional dual to the researcher-side
methodology in §3.

### The platform T&S team model

Modern platform T&S teams typically include:

- **Policy** — definitions of prohibited behavior, written and maintained
  with legal and product input.
- **Operations / enforcement** — frontline content reviewers, often
  contracted at scale (the Cognizant / Accenture / TaskUs model);
  trust-and-safety operations leads inside the platform.
- **Threat Intelligence / Adversarial Threat / Integrity** — the
  investigative arm. Conducts attribution, writes the takedown reports,
  liaises with researchers and law enforcement. The Meta Adversarial
  Threat team (built by Nathaniel Gleicher 2017–2024) is the canonical
  reference. The OpenAI Intelligence and Investigations team (Ben Nimmo's
  current org) is the contemporary AI-vendor analog.
- **Engineering** — content classifiers, account-network analyzers, CIB
  detection pipelines, automated content moderation.
- **Data Science / Research** — counterfactual modeling, harm
  measurement, intervention A/B testing.
- **Partnerships / External Affairs** — relationships with fact-
  checkers, civil society, government, academia. Manages the
  CIB-takedown-disclosure interface (the *publish-the-report* pattern).
- **Legal** — content-takedown decisions with legal implications,
  cross-border legal-process compliance.

A modern platform CIB takedown typically involves: detection (often a
combination of automated systems + researcher tipoff + internal
investigation), investigation (network reconstruction, attribution work,
content collection), legal review, removal action (account deletion,
ban, reduced distribution), and *public disclosure* (the now-canonical
Meta / OpenAI / Google TAG quarterly-or-as-needed write-up). The
disclosure is the bargain with researchers and civil society: the
platform takes action and publishes enough detail for external
verification.

### Cross-platform CIB networks

The most-difficult-to-disrupt operations operate across multiple
platforms. Doppelganger spans X, Facebook, Telegram, fake news sites,
and email. Spamouflage operates across YouTube, X, Facebook, TikTok,
Reddit, blogspot, and niche platforms. Each platform sees only its slice;
attribution and full-scope mapping require cross-platform coordination.

The historical coordination mechanisms — *Election Integrity Partnership*
2020, *Virality Project* 2021–2022, *Aspen Cybersecurity Group* — have
been substantially weakened by 2024 political pressure. The platform-to-
platform coordination has reverted to bilateral and informal channels;
the academic-side aggregation has thinned. Researchers now reconstruct
cross-platform networks from public-side data plus what each platform
chooses to disclose.

### Transparency reports — claims vs. reality

Most major platforms publish quarterly or semi-annual transparency
reports covering content removal, account actions, government requests,
and (for some) CIB takedowns. The reports vary substantially in detail.
Best-in-class (as of 2026): Meta Adversarial Threat Reports include
attribution, technique discussion, named operations. Worst-in-class:
X reports went substantially less detailed after 2022. TikTok reports
have improved over time. YouTube and Google TAG quarterly Dragonbridge
posts are detailed for Chinese operations specifically.

Treat transparency reports as *primary sources for the platform's
behavior* and *partial sources for the operational reality* — they
disclose what the platform chose to disclose, not the full enforcement
landscape.

### API access shutdowns — the 2023 researcher-access regression

Twitter/X eliminated free research-tier API access in February 2023 and
substantially restricted paid access. Reddit announced API pricing
changes June 2023 that effectively eliminated academic research access.
Both decisions sharply reduced external researcher capacity to
reconstruct platform-side activity. The 2024 election cycle was the
first major US election conducted in a research-API-degraded
environment.

The EU DSA Article 40 *Data Access for Vetted Researchers* provision
attempts to restore some access; implementation has been partial and
contested. EU-vetted researcher access is restricted to EU-jurisdiction
research questions, which excludes most US-focused work. The structural
state in May 2026: less external research capacity than 2020, more
internal-only research, and a corresponding shift toward platform-
disclosed-only public knowledge.

### The Stanford Internet Observatory wind-down

A specific institutional cautionary tale worth naming. SIO operated
2019–June 2024 under Alex Stamos and (from late 2022) Renée DiResta.
Its 2020-cycle work (*Long Fuse*) and 2021–2022 work (*Virality Project*)
were the largest US-academic election-and-vaccine disinformation research
programs. In 2023, congressional subpoenas (House Judiciary Select
Subcommittee on the Weaponization of the Federal Government),
defamation lawsuits, FOIA-style document demands, and sustained
harassment of researchers (DiResta and others received explicit threats,
some involving private-investigator surveillance) imposed millions of
dollars of legal costs and personal burden. The Supreme Court vindicated
SIO's underlying activity in *Murthy v. Missouri* (June 2024) — but by
that point Stanford's institutional decision had been made. The lab
wound down to skeleton staff in June 2024; senior researchers dispersed
(DiResta to Georgetown McCourt, Stamos to SentinelOne CISO, others to
various positions). The Harvard Shorenstein Technology and Social
Change Research Project under Joan Donovan was similarly wound down in
2023, with Donovan moving to Boston University.

The functional consequence: less independent academic research capacity,
fewer rigorous public CIB write-ups, more reliance on platform-
disclosed-only knowledge. Whether the displacement is durable is an open
question for 2026–2028.

### The "platform CIB takedown + publish CIB report" pattern

The pattern that became canonical 2018–2024:

1. Platform detects coordinated activity (internal + researcher tipoff).
2. Internal investigation; attribution to actor where possible.
3. Removal of accounts, assets, pages.
4. Public disclosure with technical detail (names of operations, attributed
   actor, sample content, technique discussion).
5. Coordination with peer platforms for cross-platform takedowns.
6. Cooperation with relevant law enforcement and researchers.

The pattern works when (a) the platform has resourced an Adversarial Threat
team, (b) leadership is willing to publicly attribute, (c) external
researchers can verify. After 2022, (c) has weakened; the *takedown-and-
disclosure* pattern persists at Meta and Google but is less consistently
practiced at X.

Cross-link `anti_fraud_abuse_engineering_expertise.md` §7-§8 — the operational
mechanics of fake-account detection and content-abuse enforcement overlap
heavily with CIB enforcement. The framings differ (fraud-focused vs.
narrative-focused) but the engineering stacks (account-network analysis,
behavioral fingerprinting, classifier training, evaluation pipelines) are
the same.

## 8. OSINT Methodology for Influence-Ops Research

The toolkit and discipline for researcher-side investigation. Most of the
public CIB knowledge corpus has been produced via these methods.

### Account-network graph reconstruction

The foundational technique (§3). Starting from a seed account or set of
seed accounts (typically a tip from a platform takedown announcement, a
fact-checker, or a journalist), expand outward through reciprocal
engagement, shared content, and topical clustering. Build the graph;
render it; identify the densely-connected cluster; collect content from
the cluster.

Tools:

- **Graphika ATLAS** (commercial; the dominant CIB-monitoring platform).
- **Gephi** (open-source visualization; the academic workhorse).
- **Cytoscape** (originally biological network visualization; widely
  reused in social-network analysis).
- **Maltego** (CTI-side with custom transforms for platform-API and
  WHOIS data).
- **NodeXL** (Microsoft Research-derived Excel plugin for social-network
  analysis).
- **Hoaxy** (Indiana OSoMe; for low-credibility-content spread tracking).
- **Hamilton 2.0** (German Marshall Fund; for tracking state-aligned
  accounts).

### Content forensics

Per-asset verification of content authenticity.

- **Reverse-image search.** TinEye (longest-running, broadest historical
  index), Google Lens (best for in-frame text recognition), Yandex (best
  for face-recognition; less politically constrained than Western
  engines), Bing Image Search, Berify. Use multiple engines —
  characteristics differ.
- **Reverse-video search.** InVID-WeVerify (browser plugin; the de
  facto tool), Berify, Forensically.
- **Metadata extraction.** exiftool, ExifTool GUI, Forensically online,
  Adobe Bridge. C2PA-manifest extraction tools (c2patool, Verify by
  Adobe).
- **Image forensics.** Error-level analysis (ELA); shadow-direction
  analysis; perspective-consistency analysis; manipulation-detection ML
  (Hive Moderation, Reality Defender, Sensity).
- **Video forensics.** Frame extraction (ffmpeg) for per-frame analysis;
  geolocation from frame content; chronolocation from sun position,
  weather, vegetation, vehicles, fashion.

### Linguistic analysis

- **Stylometry.** Burrows Delta, Mendenhall function-word distribution,
  modern transformer-embedding-based clustering. Free tools: JGAAP,
  Stylo (R package), per-domain custom analysis.
- **Translation-artifact detection.** Manual inspection by native
  speakers remains the gold standard. Specific MT-fingerprint patterns
  are well-documented in OSINT methodology literature.
- **Named-entity overlap.** Standard NLP tooling (spaCy, Stanford NER,
  HuggingFace pipelines).
- **Topic modeling.** LDA via gensim; BERTopic for embedding-based
  topic modeling; Top2Vec.

### Temporal coordination analysis

- **Posting-time histograms.** Plot per-account, per-cluster, and per-
  hashtag time distributions. Diagnostic shapes: machine-flat (constant
  rate), machine-bimodal (operator timezone working hours), organic-
  diurnal (single hump at user-local-time evening peak).
- **Synchronization analysis.** Pairwise correlation of posting times
  across accounts. CIB clusters show high pairwise correlation; organic
  communities do not.
- **Burst detection.** Identify and characterize coordinated bursts
  (multiple accounts posting on a narrow topic within a narrow window).

### Cross-platform pivots

The same operator often operates across multiple platforms. Pivots:

- **Username reuse.** Same handle across X, Telegram, Instagram, Reddit,
  TikTok. Frequently sloppy operator-OPSEC.
- **Profile-photo reuse.** Reverse-image-search across the platform's
  profile-photo image to find sister accounts.
- **Linked-URLs.** Bio links pointing to the same Linktree, same blog,
  same Telegram channel.
- **Content cross-posting.** Same posts appearing on multiple platforms
  with characteristic small-edit signatures (e.g., slight rewordings
  per-platform suggesting per-platform-tuned LLM regeneration).
- **Operational-tradecraft signatures.** Timezone signatures, language
  artifacts, narrative consistency.

### The Bellingcat methodology

The discipline that distinguishes Bellingcat-style work from generic
"Internet sleuthing": every claim is traceable to a primary source; every
finding is independently verifiable; methodology is published alongside
findings so peer-review is possible; investigators acknowledge what they
do not know and what evidence would falsify the finding. *Open-source
intelligence* in this rigorous sense is closer to court-of-law
evidentiary standards than to internet-rumor verification.

Bellingcat's online toolkit (`bellingcat.gitbook.io/toolkit`) is the
de facto practitioner manual; their training programs and case studies
(MH17, Skripal, IS chemical attacks, etc.) are reference cases. Cross-
link `threat_intel_methodology_expertise.md` §7 (OSINT discipline as
applied to technical intel) — same methodological backbone, different
substrate.

### Operational safety

Researcher-side OPSEC matters. Lessons from the 2018–2024 corpus:

- Use dedicated investigation accounts; do not log in from personal
  devices to operator-side accounts.
- Compartmentalize investigations; do not chain operations through a
  single browser profile.
- Anticipate harassment, doxing, and lawfare. The DiResta / Donovan
  experience demonstrates that high-profile researchers attract
  sustained adversarial response. Personal-account hardening, legal
  pre-positioning, and institutional support are not optional.
- Joint reporting (with a peer institution or journalism partner) is
  defensively useful — distributes the legal and reputational target.

## 9. Detection — Platform-Side and Researcher-Side

The two perspectives on the same problem. Platform-side detection is
internal and high-fidelity but black-boxed; researcher-side detection is
external and lower-fidelity but verifiable.

### Platform-side detection

Operational substrate:

- **Behavioral anomaly detection.** Graph-based clustering (DBSCAN /
  HDBSCAN over engagement-graph node features); temporal-anomaly
  scoring (posting-time distribution divergence from baseline);
  velocity-anomaly scoring (engagement growth exceeding baseline
  patterns); content-overlap clustering (LSH-based near-duplicate
  detection over post text).
- **Account-lifecycle anomaly detection.** Sudden activation of
  long-dormant accounts; sudden topic-pivot in established accounts
  (potential hijack); rapid follower-acquisition exceeding organic
  baselines; cohort-creation-time clustering (many accounts created
  within a narrow window).
- **Content classifiers.** ML models trained on labeled CIB-content
  examples; per-language classifiers; per-topic classifiers (election-
  integrity, vaccine, geopolitical conflicts).
- **Provenance verification.** C2PA manifest verification on uploaded
  media; flagging of unprovenanced political-context media.
- **Cross-platform signal sharing.** Bilateral and multilateral
  industry-coordination channels (Trust and Safety Professional
  Association forums, T&S working groups). Highly informal in 2026
  due to legal-exposure concerns following SIO experience.

### Researcher-side detection

Operational substrate:

- **Public-API monitoring.** Where API access still exists (Reddit-
  limited, X-limited, Telegram-public-channels, public Bluesky firehose,
  Mastodon firehose), scrape, store, analyze. Most academic work
  relies on this with substantial post-2023 degradation.
- **Snowball sampling.** From a seed set of known-aligned accounts,
  expand through engagement to identify cluster. Methodologically
  well-established; biased by seed selection (which is itself a
  research-design choice).
- **Cross-platform pivots** (§8).
- **Open-source-attribution** methodology. Triangulate across multiple
  sources of evidence; third-party verification; peer review through
  draft-sharing or pre-publication review with adversarial colleagues
  (the steelman discipline — cross-link
  `steelman_doctrine_expertise.md`).

### AI-generated content detection

The arms race. State-of-the-art (May 2026):

- **Text-detector** accuracy on contemporary state-of-the-art LLM
  output: 60–75% on uncontaminated text; drops to 30–50% on
  paraphrased or otherwise post-processed text. DetectGPT (Mitchell
  et al., 2023; perturbation-based likelihood-curvature test), GLTR
  (MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab; visualization-based human-augmented
  detection), OpenAI's withdrawn classifier (deprecated as
  unreliable), Hugging Face transformer-based detectors (e.g.,
  *fakespot-ai*, *Hello-SimpleAI*), GPTZero (commercial), Originality.ai
  (commercial).
- **Image-detector** accuracy on contemporary state-of-the-art image
  generators: ~70% on raw output; drops sharply on post-processed
  images. Reality Defender, Hive Moderation, Sensity AI, Optic
  (commercial); Intel FakeCatcher (heart-rate-from-pixel-color
  detection on faces). DARPA SemaFor outputs through DSRI.
- **Voice-detector** accuracy: similar 70–80% range on contemporary
  voice-clone output; drops on low-bitrate phone-call audio. Pindrop,
  Resemble AI, ElevenLabs' own detector (limited; trained on its own
  output).
- **Video-detector** accuracy: hardest modality. ~60% on contemporary
  Sora / Veo 3 / Runway outputs.

The structural problem: detectors are trained on past generator
output; generators improve faster than detector retraining cycles;
the defender lag is 6–18 months structurally. Watermarking
(Kirchenbauer et al., ICML 2023 for text; image-side counterparts
from Google SynthID and Adobe Content Credentials) is the more
durable defense — embedded at generation time, harder to remove,
but requires generator-side cooperation.

Cross-link `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §10
(watermarking) and §12 (model-output classification).

### Sigma-shape rules for influence operations

The detection-engineering pattern (cross-link
`detection_engineering_expertise.md`) ports to influence-ops detection:

- **Rule:** a structured pattern with hypothesis, observable signals,
  and false-positive analysis.
- **Hypothesis:** what behavior or content this rule detects (e.g.,
  "coordinated amplification of <NARRATIVE> by accounts with shared
  bio-link to <URL>").
- **Observables:** the platform-side or researcher-side signals
  (post-text-Jaccard-similarity above threshold; account-creation-time
  clustering; shared bio-URL).
- **False-positive analysis:** what organic activity could match
  (legitimate coordinated marketing campaigns; fan communities).
- **Evolution:** rules require maintenance under adversary adaptation —
  the operator notices the rule, changes tradecraft, the rule fails.

The DISARM framework's per-technique catalog provides the canonical
rule-hypothesis lexicon. The MISP and OpenCTI platforms can store
influence-ops detection rules with STIX-DISARM bindings.

## 10. Defensive Patterns for Organizations

For corporate-security and product teams whose remit includes
influence-ops adjacency.

### Brand monitoring for impersonation

Continuous monitoring across platforms for: accounts impersonating
brand/executives; lookalike domains; phishing infrastructure leveraging
brand-impersonation; coordinated review-bombing; narrative-disparagement
campaigns. Vendors: ZeroFox, BrandShield, Recorded Future Brand
Intelligence, Mandiant Digital Threat Monitoring, PhishLabs. In-house:
domain-monitoring (Domaintools, ZETALYTICS), social-listening (Brandwatch,
Talkwalker), dedicated investigation function. Maintain standing
relationships with platform-T&S brand-protection teams for
priority-escalation takedowns.

### Executive personal-account hardening

Public-facing executives are durable targets for impersonation, swatting,
deepfake-hostage scenarios, and account compromise:

- MFA everywhere with hardware tokens (YubiKey, Titan), not SMS.
- Sensitive-account segregation — separate devices, password managers,
  recovery emails.
- Social-media OPSEC training — voice/video samples in public posts
  become voice-cloning training material; geo-tagging discipline;
  family-account compartmentalization.
- Family-extension hardening — spouses, children, parents are pivot
  points; in scope.
- Pre-drafted crisis-comms responses + pre-arranged platform escalations.

Cross-link `appsec_program_building_expertise.md`.

### Employee training for deepfake-voice attacks

The Arup case is the canonical lesson. Defensive controls:

- **Out-of-band verification of wire-transfer authorization** —
  mandatory callback to a known number (not the number in the message);
  pre-shared word verification; multi-approver workflows above
  thresholds.
- **Voice-clone-aware awareness** — recognize the pattern (executive
  urgency + secrecy directive + novel transaction + deepfake video).
- **Slow-roll procedures** — explicit organizational permission to
  pause and verify even when the deepfake is convincing. The Arup
  staffer reported initial suspicion that was overridden by the
  apparent authenticity of the video call; culture must permit
  overriding apparent authority in favor of verification procedure.
- **Pre-incident tabletop exercises** — finance, IT, exec teams walk
  through a deepfake-CFO scenario before it happens.

### Crisis communication playbooks

Categories warranting a playbook: brand-impersonation campaign;
synthetic-media incident (deepfake executive, fabricated product-fault
video); coordinated narrative attack; insider-leak with synthetic
framing; market-manipulation event (pump-and-dump). For each: response
team (security + legal + comms + exec); decision tree (verify-and-deny,
verify-and-respond, ignore-as-amplification-risk); platform-escalation
contacts; pre-approved messaging; legal-action thresholds.

### Engagement with platform T&S for takedown

Identify per-platform takedown POCs; maintain submission templates with
evidence-package conventions; track response times and escalation paths;
reciprocate with intel sharing where appropriate.

### Pre-bunking and inoculation

The Roozenbeek / van der Linden research (Cambridge SDM Lab) provides
the strongest empirical evidence for *pre-emptive* defense: exposing
populations to weakened manipulation techniques confers durable
resistance (*Bad News* game; Roozenbeek et al. 2020 cross-cultural
study). Operationally:

- Integrate pre-bunking content into security-awareness training
  ("this is what a deepfake-voice scam looks like" before the scam
  hits).
- Platforms: surface short-form prebunking content before exposure to
  manipulation categories (Google Jigsaw + YouTube 2022 EU deployment
  is the worked example).
- Civic-orgs and electoral jurisdictions: prebunking ahead of cycles
  has measurable effect.

*Technique-specific* prebunking (e.g., the "fake-experts" pattern)
scales better than *issue-specific* prebunking.

### C2PA adoption for owned media

Sign all owned media at production time with C2PA manifests; route
through C2PA-supporting workflows (Adobe Creative Cloud, Microsoft
Designer); protect organizational signing keys under HSM-and-policy
controls; verify on receipt in sensitive workflows (M&A communications,
executive content, official statements). Adoption is asymmetric — most
consumers won't check manifests for years — but for *contested* media
moments, a C2PA-signed-from-camera-to-publication chain is the
difference between "we say it's real" and "here is cryptographic
provenance."

### Code review checklist for trust-and-safety adjacent products

For internal teams building products that touch attribution, labeling,
content moderation, or trust:

1. **Provenance integrity.** Code paths that handle C2PA manifests must
   preserve the cryptographic integrity end-to-end; manifest-stripping
   bugs are a real category. Verify on input; preserve on intermediate
   processing; re-sign on output if the platform is a producer.
2. **Label durability.** Synthetic-media labels that can be stripped by
   normal user actions (cropping, format conversion, screen-recording)
   are weaker than they appear in product specs. Label durability is a
   product-acceptance criterion.
3. **Attribution-claim safety.** Surfaces that label content with
   attribution claims ("AI-generated," "from <SOURCE>") need legal
   review for libel exposure and product review for FP cost. False
   positives carry user-trust cost.
4. **Researcher-API safety.** Endpoints that expose data for vetted-
   researcher access (DSA Article 40 path) require data-minimization
   review, abuse-resistance review, and audit-trail compliance.
5. **CIB-classifier output handling.** Classifiers used in enforcement
   actions produce regulator-disclosable outputs; logging, retention,
   and explanation chains matter.
6. **Adversarial-robustness review.** Detection systems will be probed
   by sophisticated operators; the threat model must include
   evasion-resistant design.
7. **Cross-jurisdictional consistency.** Same content may be
   permissible in one jurisdiction and prohibited in another; the
   product must handle this consistently with policy.
8. **Appeals and false-positive recovery.** Misattributed CIB labels
   need a working appeals path; the appeals process must be measured
   and audited.
9. **Provenance privacy.** C2PA manifests can leak operator-side info
   (camera serial numbers, geolocation if recorded, edit-history
   actor identity). Privacy review of manifest contents is required.
10. **Watermark robustness.** Watermarked content that survives only
    naive use is theater; the watermark scheme should survive
    typical user transformations (compression, format change,
    moderate paraphrase). Cross-link
    `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §10.
11. **Election-integrity sensitivity.** Products that operate near
    elections require additional scrutiny — accuracy claims, latency
    targets, false-positive cost on candidate content all become
    politically consequential.
12. **Sunset and audit.** Inauthentic-account detection criteria
    should be auditable, periodically reviewed for drift, and
    documented for regulator inquiry. Internal documentation of
    classifier change history is mandatory under the DSA-VLOP
    posture.

## 11. Regulatory and Policy Landscape

The regulatory regime around influence operations is the most-active
policy substrate in the field. State of play, May 2026.

### EU Digital Services Act (DSA)

Adopted October 2022; in force February 2024 for Very Large Online
Platforms (VLOPs) and Very Large Online Search Engines (VLOSEs); fully
applicable to all in-scope intermediaries since February 2024. Imposes:

- **Article 14** transparency and content-moderation obligations.
- **Article 26** advertising transparency.
- **Article 27** recommender-system transparency.
- **Article 34/35** systemic-risk identification and mitigation for
  VLOPs / VLOSEs (the influence-ops-relevant articles).
- **Article 40** vetted-researcher data-access provision.

The **DSA Code of Practice on Disinformation** was integrated into the
DSA framework on 1 July 2025 (Commission decision February 2025).
Signatories — Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, Bing, TikTok, YouTube,
Google Search — convert voluntary commitments into auditable evidence
for DSA Article 35 compliance. Annual audit cycles include disinformation
commitments from July 2025 onward.

Enforcement: VLOP/VLOSE non-compliance carries fines up to 6% of global
turnover. The Commission has opened proceedings against multiple VLOPs
since 2024.

### EU AI Act

Adopted May 2024; phased in 2024–2027. Influence-ops-relevant provisions:

- **Article 50** — transparency obligations for AI systems that
  generate synthetic content. Providers must mark output as
  AI-generated in machine-readable format. Deployers of deepfakes must
  disclose unless the use is authorized by law or constitutes artistic
  / satirical / fictional work (in which case more limited disclosure
  applies).
- **Article 50(2)** explicitly mentions watermarks, metadata-based
  identifiers, and provenance tools as appropriate technical solutions.
- **Code of Practice on Marking and Labelling AI-Generated Content** —
  drafting underway as of May 2026; expected adoption May–June 2026 in
  advance of the August 2026 transparency-obligations enforcement
  date.

Penalties for prohibited AI practices: up to EUR 35 million or 7% of
global turnover. Other major infringements: up to EUR 15 million or 3%
of turnover.

### Other regulatory regimes

- **UK Online Safety Act 2023** — statutory regime imposing duties on
  platforms regarding illegal content (incitement, coordinated harassment)
  and harmful-to-children content; Ofcom is the regulator. Less direct on
  disinformation than DSA. Enforcement ramping 2024–2026.
- **NIST AI RMF 1.0** (January 2023) and *AI 600-1* (July 2024) — US-
  government voluntary framework for AI risk management; 600-1 covers
  generative-AI risks including synthetic-media production. Cross-link
  `llm_agent_security_expertise.md`.
- **California AB 730** (2019, deepfake-near-election prohibition, narrowed
  by First Amendment injunction); **AB 2655, 2839, 2355** (2023–2024,
  political-AI disclosure). Multiple US states enacted post-2023 deepfake-
  disclosure laws targeting election content; constitutional contours
  contested.
- **US Section 230** — debate ongoing 2017–2026; multiple SCOTUS cases
  (*Gonzalez v. Google* 2023, *Taamneh* 2023, *Moody / Paxton NetChoice*
  2024) addressed adjacent issues. *Murthy v. Missouri* (June 2024) ruled
  against plaintiffs on government-platform coordination — vindicated SIO's
  underlying activity, came too late to save the lab.
- **DARPA SemaFor / RED** — *RED (Refute and Expose Discord)* (2024–)
  extends SemaFor to narrative-attack modeling.
- **Election-cycle special regimes** — France's 48-hour pre-vote
  information blackout (contained Macron Leaks 2017); EU *Political
  Advertising Regulation* (2024); fragmented US per-state regimes.

### The SIO wind-down as a policy lesson

The Stanford and Harvard institutional disinformation programs (SIO,
Shorenstein Tech & Social Change) were wound down 2023–2024 under political
and legal pressure their institutions assessed as not worth bearing despite
ultimate legal vindication. Lesson: research institutions in politically-
contested spaces require pre-positioned defenses (legal counsel,
communications, donor relationships, peer coalitions) anticipating the
*cost of defending* the activity, not just substantive merit. Donor
consortia (Knight, MacArthur, Hewlett, Omidyar) have shifted post-2024
toward distributed research networks vs. centralized programs. The
Information Integrity Lab at Georgetown (DiResta's institutional home) is
the deliberate alternative model — public-policy school with stronger
institutional infrastructure for politically-contested research.

## 12. Variant Analysis — Under-Explored Areas

### AI-agent-driven influence operations

The next frontier. As frontier-LLM agents (OpenAI Operator, Anthropic
Computer Use, Google Mariner) acquire browser-operation capabilities, the
per-account cost of inauthentic operation collapses. An agent can manage
hundreds of inauthentic accounts with per-account persona consistency,
respond in real-time, adapt narrative based on engagement, generate
per-account stylometric variation. Defensive response: agent-vs-agent
detection ("robot turing test"), behavioral-anomaly detection on agent-
driven activity, platform-level rate-limiting, mandatory verification for
political-context content. Cross-link `llm_agent_security_expertise.md`.
Empirical signal: OpenAI's *Disrupting Malicious Uses of AI* reports
document several disrupted partially-agent-driven operations by 2024–2025.

### Financial-market influence operations

High-value sub-class. May 2023 Pentagon explosion fake image briefly moved
the S&P 500; synthetic press-release attacks (fabricated SEC filings,
corporate statements) deployed against individual companies; pump-and-dump
via Telegram/Discord trading communities. Controls for trading firms: C2PA
signing of corporate communications; pre-approved channel monitoring;
synthetic-media awareness training for trading-floor staff; coordination
with FINRA, SEC, and exchange T&S teams.

### Insider-threat influence operations

A category bridging insider-threat and influence-ops: employee posting
harmful narrative through official channels (hijacked corporate account,
unauthorized press release, malicious customer-support response). The 2023
*Twitter Files* releases are a partial example — internal documents leaked
with specific framing intended to create policy effects. Whether the
framing-and-amplification side is "influence operation" or "transparency
journalism" is partly a matter of stance; operational mechanics overlap.

### Cross-platform amplification mechanics

TikTok → X → Reddit chains and similar. A narrative starts on one platform,
gets amplified across, arrives at a third pre-validated by perceived organic
spread. Telegram-to-X and Discord-to-X chains particularly operational for
state-aligned actors. Cross-platform pivot methodology (§8) is the analyst's
lever; *enforcement* response requires platform-to-platform coordination,
structurally weak in 2026.

### Synthetic media in real-time video calls

The Arup case is canonical. Real-time deepfake injection on live video
calls is technically possible at modest cost as of 2026; limiting factor is
operator skill, not technology. Cross-link `webrtc_realtime_comms_expertise.md`
§6. Controls beyond procedural callback verification: in-band challenge
protocols (head-turn, multi-blink) that current deepfake stacks struggle
with; hardware-attested camera provenance (TPM-bound C2PA); pre-shared
keyword authentication for high-value transactions.

### AI-deepfake disclosure litigation

Growing 2024–2026 case category. Defamation lawsuits against deepfake
producers; SCOTUS engagement on First Amendment contours of
deepfake-disclosure statutes; private-suit deepfake-intimate-image cases
(largest single category by per-victim count; not the focus of this doc).
Legal architecture is forming; expect significant doctrinal development
through 2028.

### Influence operations against AI training data

Speculative but plausible. If state actors inject biased content into
training corpora of frontier LLMs (Common Crawl pollution, Wikipedia
manipulation, Reddit / Stack Exchange poisoning), they can shape model
behavior at the substrate level — past per-prompt to per-model bias.
Anthropic, OpenAI, Google have discussed training-data integrity research;
specific incidents not publicly documented at scale as of May 2026. Cross-
link `adversarial_ml_model_security_expertise.md` §9 (data poisoning).

### Sovereign AI and information sovereignty

Taiwan, France, EU, India, Singapore pursuing sovereign-AI capability
partly as response to the information-warfare landscape. Premise: relying
entirely on US-vendor LLMs creates an information-environment dependency.
Taiwan's TAIDE is an early example; Mistral (France) and EC AI initiatives
broader-scope. Downstream influence-ops implications unresolved.

### LLM-as-fact-checker substrates

Platforms piloting LLM-driven fact-checking-at-scale (X's *Grok*
community-notes integration is most-visible as of 2026). Empirical question
— do LLM fact-checkers improve or degrade information quality? — open;
Cambridge SDM Lab and others suggest mixed results. Emerging consensus:
LLMs are useful *triage* for fact-checking workflows (flagging for human
review) but unreliable as *adjudicators*.
